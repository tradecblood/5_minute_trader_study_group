The Core Theory of Options Trading

Options are financial contracts that give you the right—but not the obligation—to buy or sell a stock at a fixed price (Strike Price) within a specific timeframe. 

Every contract controls 100 shares of the underlying stock.

Call Options: The Right to BUY

When you buy a Call, you are betting the stock price will rise.

The Mechanism: You lock in a purchase price. If the stock skyrockets, your contract allows you to buy shares at that cheap, locked-in strike price. This makes your contract more valuable and attractive to buyers when you sell it. 

In-the-Money (ITM): The stock price is higher than your strike price. Your contract holds a built-in discount.

Profit Formula at Expiration:Profit = (Stock Price - Strike Price - Premium Paid) x 100

Example: If you buy a $100 Strike Call for a $5 premium, your break-even is $105.

 If the stock hits $120, your profit is:($120 - $100 - $5) x 100 = $1,500 profit

************************

Put Options: The Right to SELL

When you buy a Put, you are betting the stock price will fall.

The Mechanism: You lock in a selling price. If the stock crashes, your contract allows you to sell shares at that high, locked-in strike price—even if the market price is much lower. This makes your contract more valuable and attractive to buyers when you sell it. 

In-the-Money (ITM): The stock price is lower than your strike price. Your contract holds a built-in premium selling advantage.

Profit Formula at Expiration:Profit = (Strike Price - Stock Price - Premium Paid) x 100

Example: If you buy a $100 Strike Put for a $5 premium, your break-even is $95. If the stock drops to $80, your profit is:($100 - $80 - $5) x 100 = $1,500 profit
